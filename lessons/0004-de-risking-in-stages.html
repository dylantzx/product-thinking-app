Risk, and building in stages

Before you build the whole app, find the biggest way it could fail and test that first.

Every product has risk

The IDG says if a project seems entirely risk-free, someone isn't looking hard enough. It names three types:

Market risk
Will anyone use this, and will it actually solve the problem?
Technical risk
Is this too complex or costly to build, relative to impact?
Team risk
Do we have the right capabilities to pull this off? The IDG suggests combining policy, operations and technology expertise, with clear accountability for each role.

Four stages of de-risking

Build in stages. At every stage users have something functional, and you are learning.

1 · Proof of concept

One segment, one use case.

2 · Proof of value

Can it scale?

3 · Scale

Reach as many users as possible.

4 · Maturity

Get better over time.

The IDG shows Amazon (books), Facebook (one university), Airbnb (founders' spare room), Netflix (DVD rentals), Grab (taxis only) and SG Health Booking (FormSG plus an Excel sheet) as products that started small and scaled what worked.

Case study: health appointment booking

Hypothesis: make vaccine booking easier and more people will get vaccinated. Key risk: market risk. In a two-week A/B test with one variable, the control group got a standard letter and had to find a clinic: 0 appointments booked. The treatment group got a QR code linking to the booking flow: 24 appointments, and users shared the link unprompted. The first version was not scalable. The IDG's point is that it was the lowest-cost way to validate the approach.
