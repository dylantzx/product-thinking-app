Lesson 5 · Product Thinking · ~10 min

A good customer experience

Your app solves a real problem. Now decide what using it should feel like.

Experience is not optional

IDG module 6 argues that good customer experience matters especially for essential public services, where people may have no alternative. The question moves from "does it solve the problem?" to "what will using it feel like?"

Four design principles

The product thinks for the user
If it needs a lengthy tutorial, that is a signal about the design, not about your users.
Solve specific frustrations
Each feature should address a real, named pain point, not just add capability.
Make smart trade-offs
Get the essentials right first, and build a roadmap for everything else.
Especially when it is mandatory
When citizens cannot go elsewhere, the standard for good design is higher, not lower.

IDG examples: ParkingSG auto-calculates fees, lets you extend sessions remotely and refunds partial sessions. Redeem.sg served three user groups (organisers, consumers, merchants) and got the essentials right first.

The 11-star framework

Imagine an experience at each star level, using the IDG's door-knocking story: 1 star is you knock and no one answers; 5 stars is the door opens and you are let in, which is the baseline, not enough; 7 stars is the host knows you surf and leaves a board and local restaurant tips; 11 stars is Elon Musk meeting you at the gate and taking you to space. The 10 and 11-star levels are the prompt, not the goal. They push you past "good enough" to find what is both ambitious and achievable, somewhere between 5 and 11.

Check yourself

Try it on your app

1. For your problem statement, describe what a 1, 5, 7, 9, 10 and 11-star experience looks like. 2. Ask what you can realistically deliver, and what would get you closest to that ambition.
