Metrics that measure the outcome

You have a problem statement. Pick a number that shows whether it is getting solved.

Why metrics

You wrote a problem statement. Now you need a number that tells you whether you are solving it. The IDG gives three tools: SMART, leading and lagging indicators, and the Value-Cost Ratio.

Tool 1: SMART

A good metric is Specific (a precise outcome, not a vague direction), Measurable (a concrete data point, not a feeling), Achievable (ambitious but grounded in real capacity), Relevant (it moves when the problem is being solved) and Time-bound (anchored to a timeframe). If you cannot make it SMART, the IDG says your problem statement may be too broad.

IDG example: "Improve click-through rate from 20% to 25% by end of Q3", not "make the website better".

Tool 2: Leading and lagging indicators

Leading indicators are easy to measure and give fast feedback. Lagging indicators sit closer to the outcome and verify real impact. The IDG's example chain for a teachers' tool runs from "teachers onboarded daily", to "used a specific feature", to "crafted a message in 30 minutes", to "campaign approved in 2 days", to the outcome "time saved per teacher". Low adoption in week two tells you it probably is not saving anyone time, so you need not wait until quarter-end.

Tool 3: Value-Cost Ratio (VCR)

VCR is units of value generated per dollar spent. IDG example: 100,000 messages created ÷ $10,000 platform cost = 10 messages per dollar. It does not tell you whether a number is good or bad in isolation, but it sharpens the question of what an extra dollar of investment would return. Not all public-sector value fits in dollars, so treat VCR as one input among several.

Check yourself

Try it on your app

1. Turn your outcome from lesson 1 into a SMART metric. 2. List 3 to 5 indicators from easiest (leading) to closest to the outcome (lagging). 3. If you can, estimate your VCR: value units divided by cost.
